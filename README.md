# Syllavane

**A native Mac writing companion that turns spoken ideas into drafts, with local AI and a voice shaped by your writing.**

Syllavane gives each book, blog, short post, or technical draft its own workspace, sources, characters, and writing memory. Describe an idea aloud or type a brief, review the resulting prose, then edit or accept it. The interface keeps writing central and puts secondary tools behind compact menus.

**Current version: 0.11.0 · experimental local release · Apple Silicon · macOS 14+**

> Syllavane is a working application under active development. Draft recovery and persistence are tested; dependable literary quality and faithful style reproduction remain goals. Review generated writing before accepting it.

## What you can do

| Area | Included |
| --- | --- |
| Writing | Speak → stop → review a draft; typed idea submission; continuous chapter conversations |
| Projects | Separate book, blog, X-post, and technical-paper workspaces; chapters nested under projects |
| Rewriting | Select a passage for a nearby Rewrite menu, or revise the whole draft; custom typed or spoken feedback |
| Review | Before/after comparison, Keep, Undo, revision history, and a brief highlight identifying changed text |
| Personalization | Writing-source analysis, manual-edit examples, approved rewrite examples, and removable project preferences |
| Characters | Aliases, uploaded portraits, trait cards, custom characteristics, and developments effective from a particular story moment |
| Story map | Character relationships with narration evidence, plus scene tiles showing character involvement |
| Sources | Import or paste material as a continuation, factual reference, or writing-style sample |
| Export | Whole manuscript or chapter PDF preview, typography presets, Markdown manuscript, and JSON project backup |
| Recovery | Autosave, daily library backups, unsent-input persistence, interrupted-audio recovery, and guarded draft replacement |

## Start writing

1. Create a project and choose its writing format. A working title is optional.
2. Add existing material through the project menu → Sources. Choose whether it continues the manuscript, supplies reference facts, or demonstrates your style.
3. Click **Speak**, describe your idea, and finish speaking. A draft is generated by default. Typed ideas use the send arrow or **⌘Return**.
4. Review the draft. Edit directly, highlight text to rewrite it, or give a change request for the whole draft.
5. Choose **Keep change** to approve a rewrite example, or **Undo change** to restore the earlier wording. Accept the draft to add it to your manuscript.
6. Keep narrating. Finish the chapter when you decide its boundary; unresolved drafts must be reviewed first.

Characters, sources, story map, revision history, and **What I’ve Learned** are available through the project menu. PDF Preview lets you choose the whole manuscript or a chapter, configure typography, and save the preview as a PDF.

## How personalization works

Each project has its own editable style guidance, source analysis, remembered preferences, and before/after examples.

- **Writing samples** supply original excerpts and observations about rhythm, narrative distance, scene construction, psychological detail, dialogue, and imagery. Source quotations support the observations; the interpretation still needs your review.
- **Manual edits** become bounded examples of your own corrections. Relevant examples are retrieved for later drafting and rewriting.
- **Approved rewrites** become style examples when you choose Keep change or accept a revised draft. A failed request or undone rewrite does not add an approved example.
- **Story facts** come from narration, structured characters, references, and author corrections. Approved AI rewrites are kept separate from factual character evidence.
- **What I’ve Learned** lets you inspect and forget examples and preferences. Examples from later story moments are excluded when drafting earlier moments.

This adapts the context sent to the local writer. It does **not** retrain model weights or guarantee that every edit improves the next draft.

## Local AI and privacy

Writing, retrieval, and English dictation run locally after the initial downloads. There is no hosted AI fallback, account requirement, telemetry, cloud sync, or Apple Intelligence dependency.

| Role | Model | Approximate download |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing | Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, Q4_K_M | 2.50 GB |
| Retrieval | all-MiniLM-L6-v2, F16 GGUF | 46 MB |
| English speech | Whisper small.en | 488 MB |

Model files total approximately **3.03 GB**. The app bundle and runtime memory are additional. The model manifest pins download revisions, file sizes, and SHA-256 checksums. Local engines bind to loopback and use per-launch authentication; the speech runtime includes an authentication patch.

Projects and revisions are stored in `~/Library/Application Support/Folio/library.sqlite`. Models, backups, logs, and recovery files live under the same application support directory. The internal `Folio` name remains stable to preserve existing data and permissions. This repository and release archives contain application code and resources, not personal libraries, manuscripts, recordings, or model weights.

## Build from source

You need an Apple Silicon Mac running macOS 14 or later, Swift 6 or newer, Xcode command-line tools, CMake, Python 3, and an internet connection for initial dependency downloads.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/vashisht7/Syllavane.git
cd Syllavane
./scripts/build.sh
open dist/Syllavane.app
```

The build downloads checksum-verified upstream engine archives, applies the local speech authentication patch, compiles the runtimes, runs the ordinary tests, builds the app, generates its icon, and ad-hoc signs the bundle. The app's Settings offers **Download & start** for models. You can also install the pinned models from the terminal:

```sh
python3 scripts/prepare-models.py
```

For an existing build, rebuild only the app and package:

```sh
swift build -c release
python3 scripts/package.py
```

The downloadable app is ad-hoc signed. Developer ID signing, notarization, and App Store distribution are not yet implemented.

## Verification

```sh
# Ordinary regression suite; no model downloads required
swift test

# Memory-safety checks
swift test --sanitize=address

# Installed-model integration tests; build/package and download models first
FOLIO_RUN_MODEL_TESTS=1 swift test --filter LocalModelIntegrationTests

# Packaged runtime and speech checks
python3 scripts/smoke-test.py
python3 scripts/speech-reliability.py
codesign --verify --deep --strict dist/Syllavane.app
```

Version 0.11 passed **77 ordinary regression checks**; two opt-in installed-model tests are skipped in the ordinary run. The new approved-rewrite integration test passed separately, including persistence after reopening. Its sample paragraph revision took **2.29 seconds** on the development Mac; this is one measurement, not a general latency guarantee.

Tests cover persistence, backup recovery, cancellation and late replies, selection-only revisions, alias ambiguity, character timelines, future-context exclusion, chapter boundaries, stream failures, audio recovery, approved learning, and failed rebuild preservation. Earlier verification also exercised five drafting fixtures and five synthesized speech fixtures, including controlled noise.

See [verification](docs/VERIFICATION.md), [architecture](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md), and [changelog](CHANGELOG.md) for details.

## Current limitations

- The local writer can produce weak prose, repetitive imagery, unsupported interpretations, or inaccurate style analysis. Validation reduces drift but cannot prove fidelity.
- Dictation supports English. Accent coverage, competing speakers, physical device interruptions, and VoiceOver need broader hands-on testing.
- Fiction recovery can require multiple model calls and take longer than a short revision.
- Recordings stop at twenty minutes; a crash can lose audio after the most recent five-second checkpoint.
- Inferred relationships need review. Evidence quotations do not prove that every interpretation is correct.
- Blog, X-post, and technical modes provide drafting guidance; there are no publishing integrations or external fact checks.
- Chapter reordering, automatic chapter suggestions, DOCX export, image generation, and advanced publication typography are not implemented.
- Verification covers one development Mac, rather than every supported macOS version or device.
